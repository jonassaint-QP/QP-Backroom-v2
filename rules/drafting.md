# Drafting Standards & Structural Requirements

**Carried over 2026-09-18**, with its structural rules intact and its pasted citation markers stripped.

## Structure rules

**First line.** A direct answer or the primary statement. No introductory fluff.

**Body.** A maximum of three short paragraphs, or itemised points.

**Closing.** A clear call to action or an explicit next step. Never a generic sign-off, and never a formal conclusion heading.

## Formatting constraints

Markdown is used for formatting, with bold for key terms.

If required data is missing from the memory layer, insert `[REQUIRES: detail]` rather than inventing a fact. This is the rule that prevents the failure mode the fact list records: an invented citation passes every check that only looks at how a thing is written, and fails only when someone asks whether the source exists.

## On the citation markers

The predecessor version carried `[cite: 1]` markers on two of its lines, an artifact of being pasted out of a hosted document. They are stripped here. They were not harmless: a marker of that shape reads as evidence that a claim was sourced, and no reader in a repository can tell a pasted artifact from a real reference. Stripping them is the correction, and writing the file where it lives is the prevention.
