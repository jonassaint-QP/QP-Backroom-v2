# Policy: Source Authority and the Precedence of the Record

**ID:** QP-POL-004
**Effective Date:** 2026-09-22
**Status:** Active

## Purpose

To fix, in writing, where a fact is allowed to come from. Every seat reads this policy on every turn, because the failure it prevents is invisible from inside a single answer.

## The rule

Four sources exist, and they are ranked. The ranking does not change with the length of a document, the number of times a claim has been repeated, or how recently a file was opened.

**1. The repository.** Governing rules and the canonical record. Version-controlled, dated, attributable. This is the only layer that can hold a rule, and the only layer where a superseded entry can be point-forwarded to its replacement.

**2. The seat's own Drive folder.** That seat's live daily state, including its operating spec, its calendar state and its signal register. Hand-editable without a commit, which is why a seat maintains it unaided.

**3. The shared rules reader file.** The consolidated roster, live state, directory map and policy set. Authoritative on what the rules say; superseded by the repository where the two disagree, and reported rather than silently resolved.

**4. Acquired corpora.** NotebookLM and anything else that grows by acquisition rather than by live operational experience. Background only.

## Acquired corpora hold no authority over current state

A notebook has no native tools. It cannot write a tombstone, it cannot clear a scheduled task, it cannot observe a state change on any platform, and it cannot date itself. It is read for exactly one purpose: long-form themes, clinical frameworks, and subcultural research that inform the substance of a piece of work. It is never read to establish what is true now.

No seat sources a claim about current state to an acquired corpus. No seat treats a passage from one as a dated instruction. No seat resolves a conflict by choosing the version it has seen more often.

## Conflict resolution

A notebook entry and a dated seat-folder entry that conflict: **the dated entry wins automatically, and without further review.**

A notebook entry and a governing rule that conflict: **the rule wins, and the conflict is reported rather than resolved in the seat's favour.**

A note about a set of records and the records themselves: **the records win. Enumerate by identifier, never from a note about the set.** This is the same failure in a different costume, and it has now produced duplicate canonical records on three consecutive dates.

## Why this is written as a wall rather than a guideline

A language model has no internal clock. Repetition reads as authority, so a retired jurisdiction documented across dozens of older briefs will outrank a single newer dated decision unless something explicitly forbids it. That is the documented mechanism by which retired footprints and legacy coordinates kept returning as live scope.

**Volume is not currency, and recency is not authority. Only the dated, attributable record is either.**

## Scope

Binds every seat, the shared rules reader file, and any Gem or assistant configured against these rules. It applies to any acquired corpus, not to one notebook, and it applies whether the corpus is attached as a source or queried directly.
