## Pre-Dispatch Clearance (mandatory)

**This brief is cleared before dispatch, not the script after it.** Editorial review clears structure first, then compliance review clears content. The render runs only when both have cleared.

**Why the gate sits here.** The render is a single run: the text model writes the script and the TTS engine voices it in the same pass, producing the mp3 and the turn array together. There is no script-only artifact waiting to be cleared afterwards, and a compliance failure found after the render does not stop publication — it only means the render was already spent. The brief is also the only artifact in this pipeline a person actually writes.

**Editorial clearance covers:** the beats and their order, the section split and its word targets, the cast and voice identifiers, the terms of art, the call to action, the exclusions, and the lane assignment per beat.

**Compliance clearance covers:** the claims made, the clinical framing, the authority references, the boundary conventions and the prohibited content — including the clinical skill names, the founder's geographic boundary, and the halted-framing list below.

**The generated turns are verified against this brief after the render**, but that is verification, not clearance. Where the output departs from a cleared brief, correct the brief and re-render. An uncleared departure is not published.

## Production Parameters

**Three-section cut.** Section One: cold open plus beats one and two. Section Two: beats three and four. Section Three: beats five and six plus the episode close. **Each section 1,800 to 2,150 words**, because the renderer requests one section per call and a thin section renders thin.

**Mid-roll breaks.** Sections One and Two close with brief somatic resets — unclamp the jaw, drop the shoulders, check the Body Dashboard, take a slow breath — rather than sign-offs or booking calls. There is **one** formal close and **one** call to action, at the end of Section Three.

**Exact call to action text, comma form:** Adjourn the Courtroom, Book Your Intake Assessment. Spoken once, on the final turn, never mid-section.

**Destination:** the secure TherapyNotes patient portal, or the base-of-page booking container. Never the site root.

**Excluded:** the Dignity Investment block, entirely — not on a brief, not on a draft, not on an intermediate pass. All unsupported numeric claims. Client-identifying detail. Carceral imagery **used of people** — do not liken listeners, clients, partners or the queer and neurodivergent communities to defendants, criminals, inmates or the prosecuted. The courtroom is the internal architecture the listener is being walked out of, and Adjourn the Courtroom is the mandated anchor, so the framework's own vocabulary is not carceral imagery in this sense and is not excluded by this line.

## Section Breakdown and Beat Assignments

**Every beat carries a named lane.** A beat with no lane arrives thin, because the second register's material gets summarised rather than written. That is the failure behind every short script to date.

### Section One: Cold Open and the Administrative Trial

#### Cold Open

**Jason — clinical lane.** Frame the high-performance post-win crash and the executive collapse that lands around 5:30 PM, introducing the point of performance where working memory drops off.

**David — editorial lane.** The grounded translation: the Email Stare, the 2:00 AM paralysis, the felt sense of a blank screen while adrenaline surges.

#### Beat One: The Administrative Double-Bind

**Jason — clinical lane.** The structural flaw of working-memory drop-off, using Russell Barkley on executive function and the point of performance. Why assigning administrative memory to one partner without external systems guarantees dropped balls.

**David — editorial lane.** The kitchen scene — holding the permission forms, setting the calendar alerts, being called a nag when reminding a partner, and taking the whole of the blame when the deadline passes.

#### Beat Two: Shame and Defensive Armor

**Jason — clinical lane.** The immediate panic response through Brené Brown on courage and shame — defensive criticism as armour against the pain of personal failure. The EFT sequence through Sue Johnson on emotional accessibility and responsiveness, where administrative friction reflects lost attunement.

**David — editorial lane.** The courtroom shift — how a missed permission form stops being about logistics and becomes an interrogation about how, when and why the prompt was delivered. The counter-argument against litigating tone over an operational breakdown.

*Section One break: brief somatic reset. Check the Body Dashboard, drop the shoulders, unclamp the jaw, one slow breath.*

### Section Two: Internal Architecture and Archetype Mirrors

#### Beat Three: The Body Dashboard Against the Auditor

**Jason — clinical lane.** The Body Dashboard as the somatic feedback loop, and the Auditor as the cold internal monitoring system. Marsha Linehan on radical acceptance as the move that lets biological reality be a fact rather than a moral failing.

**David — editorial lane.** The Auditor's cold calculation against actual somatic capacity — and the plain-language gut check on the point where the Auditor stops sounding like a bureaucrat and starts selling strategy, telling you that chaos is the only familiar fuel available.

#### Beat Four: Archetype Mirrors Across High-Performance Cycles

**Jason — clinical lane.** The archetypes across their age windows, with Alex as the primary lens for high-performance cycles and disorganized attachment — the white non-binary double-outsider frequently read as a masculine-presenting gay man. Marcus as resilient architecture, Jasper as kinetic armor, Gabe as raw intensity, Simon as rigid protocol, Ken as technical precision.

**David — editorial lane.** The archetypes as relational mirrors — how recognising whether you or your partner are running kinetic armor or technical precision de-escalates friction without requiring a rigid biographical diagnosis.

*Section Two break: brief somatic reset. Check the pulse, release the armour being held, return for the final section.*

### Section Three: Survival Architecture and Somatic Sovereignty

#### Beat Five: Survival Architecture and System Malware

**Jason — clinical lane.** The Internal Courtroom, where the self is litigated, separated cleanly from Survival Architecture, what gets built outside it to function in a neurotypical system. Garbage Code as the malformed instruction set feeding the Auditor. The F-150 as the visible execution layer. **The Ambiguity Tax is carried over here, not re-defined** — defined at Beat One. Capacity Catfishing as the structural breach. Collapse as terminus rather than strategy, and total depletion excluded for exactly that reason.

**David — editorial lane.** Parking the F-150 as a tactical upgrade in posture rather than surrender — the thing that lets the nervous system rest. Paying the Ambiguity Tax all day, and the point where it forces Capacity Catfishing out of someone who promised consistency their body could not deliver.

#### Beat Six: The Centaur Framework and Life Transformation

**Jason — clinical lane.** The Centaur as the core architecture for ADHD, neurodivergent and kinky individuals negotiating dual identity — the executive torso and the somatic engine combined, commanding the perimeter and providing a safe harbour through regulated presence.

**David — editorial lane.** The listener's actual question: why listen, and what changes. Moving from internal litigation to clinical-grade somatic sovereignty — navigating intimacy without shame, building objective scaffolding, and claiming real peace.

#### Episode Close

**Jason.** The closing synthesis — dismantling the internal prosecutor, stepping into identity-fluent care.

**David.** The closing reflection as a varied question about what becomes possible when a person stops litigating their own existence — varied, not the show's canonical question verbatim. Then the call to action, once, on the final turn: Adjourn the Courtroom, Book Your Intake Assessment.
